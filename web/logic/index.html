01 Methodology

How the position is built, hedged, and where the yield comes from.

What is it?

The Leveraged Basis Strategy is a delta-neutral carry trade on Ethereum. It earns yield from two independent sources — perpetual funding rates and stETH staking rewards — while remaining fully hedged against ETH price movements. The strategy does not take a directional bet on ETH; its return comes purely from the spread between what shorts are paid (funding + staking) and what the leveraged position costs to finance (USDC borrow interest).

How it works

  • Collateral loop (5×): Starting with 1 stETH, the strategy repeatedly deposits stETH, borrows USDC at 80% LTV, buys more stETH, and re-deposits. After 5 loops this builds a ×3.36 leveraged stETH position on-chain.
  • Delta hedge: An equal-sized short position on the ETH-USDT perpetual futures market cancels all directional ETH exposure. The portfolio is price-neutral — it neither gains nor loses from ETH price moves.
  • Funding income: Perpetual longs pay shorts when the market is net bullish (avg raw rate 15.4% APR historically). At ×3.36 leverage this amplifies to roughly 46.5% APR on posted equity.
  • Staking income: All looped stETH earns ~2.7% APR, amplified to roughly 9.1% APR on equity.
  • Cost: USDC borrow interest on the leveraged position (avg 5.6% APR including a 0.5% protocol spread), netting to roughly 13.2% APR drag on equity.

Quarterly rebalancing

At the end of each quarter the position is rebalanced: collateral, short notional, and borrow size are all rescaled to the current portfolio value. This keeps the leverage ratio constant and ensures compounding gains are fully redeployed.

Key risks

  • Negative funding: When the market turns bearish, longs pay nothing and shorts may pay longs (observed: 2022 Q3 −53.6% annualised; 2025 Q1 −35.5%).
  • Borrow rate spikes: DeFi utilisation surges can push USDC rates above funding income (notable in late 2023, when borrow cost reached ~20% annualised).
  • Liquidation risk: 80% LTV per loop leaves a buffer, but extreme price volatility or oracle manipulation could threaten collateral ratios.
  • Smart-contract risk: Lending protocol (Aave/Compound) or exchange failure/exploit.
  • Execution slippage: Modelled in the backtest at a 10% funding take-rate.
